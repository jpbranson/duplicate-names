Dedication
Thomas “Tom” Johnston
This	year’s	town	report	is	dedicated	to	Thomas	Johnston.	Tom	has	been	
active	in	many	town	organizations	throughout	the	years,	including	stints	on	
the	Select	Board	(seven	years)	and	the	school	board.		
Since	high	school,	Tom	has	been	responding	to	fires	in	town	and	served	
as	chief	of	Washington	Volunteer	Fire	Department	for	8	years.	In	addition,	
he	served	on	the	Maine	State	Prison	Fire	Department,	from	which	he	retired	
after	24	years	as	captain	and	safety	officer.	In	Washington,	Tom	still	holds	
the	rank	of	captain	on	the	fire	department	and	continues	to	educate	new	
firefighters	around	the	state.	
For	more	than	50	years,	Tom	has	been	a	member	of	the	Evening	Star	
Grange	and	has	received	the	Golden	Sheaf	Award.	He	is	a	past	grange	
master	and	plowed	the	parking	lot	for	many	years.	His	fresh	vegetable	
contributions	to	grange	exhibitions	at	Union	and	Windsor	fairs	helped	bring	
home	numerous	blue	ribbons.	Recently,	Tom	has	helped	upgrade	grange	
safety	and	prepare	for	insurance.	
Tom	has	coached	middle	school	softball	in	the	past	and	is	a	long-time	
member	of	the	Hill	&	Gully	Riders	Snowmobile	Club,	currently	serving	
as	its	treasurer	and	trails	groomer.	In	his	younger	years,	Tom	worked	at	
Medomak	Camp	and	was	a	Boy	Scout.	He	has	spent	most	of	his	life	farming,	
raising	cattle,	and	making	and	selling	hay.	He	graduated	from	Medomak	
Valley	High	School	and	went	on	to	graduate	from	University	of	Maine	at	
Orono	with	a	degree	in	forestry.	
This	summer,	Tom	and	Deb	will	mark	their	50th	wedding	anniversary.	
They	were	high	school	sweethearts	and	have	two	daughters	and	three	
grandchildren.

In Memoriam
Mary Merrifield
Mary	Merrifield	passed	away	on	December	8,	2023	at	the	age	of	79.	
She	passed	away	at	her	home,	surrounded	by	people	who	loved	her.	
She	first	came	to	Washington	in	975,	purchasing	the	home	she	lived	in	
on	Shepard	Hill	Road	in	976.	She	had	learned	about	Washington	from	an	
“old-timer”	who	frequented	the	coffee	shop	where	she	worked	in	Holbrook,	
Massachusetts.	His	stories	of	growing	up	in	Washington	intrigued	her,	and	
when	she	first	saw	the	“Home	Place”	as	she	came	to	call	it,	she	knew	that	
she	had	found	the	place	she	had	envisioned	raising	her	two	children.	She	
was	a	single	mom,	and	worked	two	and	often	three	jobs	in	order	to	realize	
her	dream	of	moving	to	Maine.	Once	here,	she	worked	doggedly	for	years	
to	create	her	beautiful	home,	doing	much	of	the	remodeling	herself.
Throughout	the	years	Mary	worked	in	various	jobs,	from	secretarial	
and	waitressing	positions	to	roofing.	Her	last	job,	as	secretary	for	the	legal	
department	at	Maine	Municipal	Association,	was	her	favorite.	While	she	
enjoyed	her	job,	she	found	her	fulfillment	in	more	creative	pursuits	-	she	
loved	sewing,	gardening,	cooking	and	photography.	She	was	a	voracious	
reader	and	had	an	extensive	library	which	she	enjoyed	sharing	with	others.	
She	also	loved	her	animals.	Over	the	years	she	had	many	treasured	dogs,	
horses,	cats	and	fowl.
Mary	was	many	things:	a	woman	of	strong	faith,	a	dedicated	mother,	
a	doting	grandmother,	a	loyal	friend,	a	hard	worker.	She	had	an	open	and	
generous	heart.	Mary	was	a	beautiful	soul	who	was	dearly	loved	and	will	
be	sorely	missed.

Discussion, and Analysis
The	following	management’s	discussion	and	analysis	of	the	Town	of	Washington,	
Maine’s	financial	performance	provides	an	overview	of	the	Town’s	financial	
activities	for	the	fiscal	year	ended	December	3,	2023.	Please	read	it	in	conjunction	
with	the	Town’s	financial	statements.
Financial Statement Overview
The	Town	of	Washington’s	basic	financial	statements	include	the	following	
components:	
)	government-wide	financial	statements,	2)	fund	financial	statements,	and	3)	
notes	to	the	financial	statements.	This	report	also	includes	required	supplementary	
information	which	consists	of	the	general	fund	budgetary	comparison	schedule,	and	
other	supplementary	information	which	includes	combining	and	other	schedules.
Basic Financial Statements
The	basic	financial	statements	include	financial	information	in	two	differing	
views:	the	government-wide	financial	statements	and	the	fund	financial	statements.	
These	basic	financial	statements	also	include	the	notes	to	financial	statements	
that	explain	in	more	detail	certain	information	in	the	financial	statements	and	also	
provide	the	user	with	the	accounting	policies	used	in	the	preparation	of	the	financial	
statements.
Government-Wide Financial Statements
The	government-wide	financial	statements	provide	a	broad	view	of	the	Town’s	
operations	in	a	manner	that	is	similar	to	private	businesses.	These	statements	
provide	both	short-term	as	well	as	long-term	information	in	regards	to	the	Town’s	
financial	position.	These	financial	statements	are	prepared	using	the	accrual	basis	of	
accounting.	This	measurement	focus	takes	into	account	all	revenues	and	expenses	
associated	with	the	fiscal	year	regardless	of	when	cash	is	received	or	paid.	The	
government-wide	financial	statements	include	the	following	two	statements:
The	Statement	of	Net	Position	-	this	statement	presents	all	of	the	government’s	
assets	and	liabilities	with	the	difference	being	reported	as	net	position.
The	Statement	of	Activities	-	this	statement	presents	information	that	shows	how	
the	government’s	net	position	changed	during	the	period.	All	changes	in	net	position	
are	reported	as	soon	as	the	underlying	event	giving	rise	to	the	change	occurs,	
regardless	of	the	timing	of	the	related	cash	flows.

one	type	of	activity.	The	type	of	activity	presented	for	the	Town	of	Washington	is:

Fund Financial Statements 
 
       A fund is a grouping of related accounts that is used to maintain control over resources that have 
been segregated for specific activities or objectives.  The Town of Washington, like other local 
governments uses fund accounting to ensure and demonstrate compliance with financial related legal 
requirements.  All of the funds of the Town of Washington are categorized as one fund type: 
governmental funds. 
 
       Governmental funds: All of the basic services provided by the Town are financed through 
governmental funds.  Governmental funds are used to account for essentially the same functions 
reported in governmental activities in the government-wide financial statements.  However, unlike the 
government-wide financial statements, the governmental fund financial statements focus on near-term 
inflows and outflows of spendable resources.  They also focus on the balance of spendable resources 
available at the end of the fiscal year.  Such information will be useful in evaluating the government’s 
near-term financing requirements.  This approach is known as the current financial resources 
measurement focus and the modified accrual basis of accounting.  Under this approach revenues are 
recorded when cash is received or when susceptible to accrual.  Expenditures are recorded when 
liabilities are incurred and due.  These statements provide a detailed short-term view of the Town’s 
finances to assist in determining whether there will be adequate financial resources available to meet 
the current needs of the Town.

thus	an	increase	in	net	position	of	$442,828.82	during	fiscal	year	2023.
Fund Financial Statements
A	fund	is	a	grouping	of	related	accounts	that	is	used	to	maintain	control	over	
resources	that	have	been	segregated	for	specific	activities	or	objectives.	The	Town	
of	Washington,	like	other	local	governments	uses	fund	accounting	to	ensure	and	
demonstrate	compliance	with	financial	related	legal	requirements.	All	of	the	funds	
of	the	Town	of	Washington	are	categorized	as	one	fund	type:	governmental	funds.
Governmental funds:	All	of	the	basic	services	provided	by	the	Town	are	financed	
through	governmental	funds.	Governmental	funds	are	used	to	account	for	essentially	
the	same	functions	reported	in	governmental	activities	in	the	government-wide	
financial	statements.	However,	unlike	the	government-wide	financial	statements,	
the	governmental	fund	financial	statements	focus	on	near-term	inflows	and	outflows	
of	spendable	resources.	They	also	focus	on	the	balance	of	spendable	resources	
available	at	the	end	of	the	fiscal	year.	Such	information	will	be	useful	in	evaluating	
the	government’s	near-term	financing	requirements.	This	approach	is	known	as	the	
current	financial	resources	measurement	focus	and	the	modified	accrual	basis	of	
accounting.	Under	this	approach	revenues	are	recorded	when	cash	is	received	or	
when	susceptible	to	accrual.	Expenditures	are	recorded	when	liabilities	are	incurred

to	assist	in	determining	whether	there	will	be	adequate	financial	resources	available	
to	meet	the	current	needs	of	the	Town.

Because	the	focus	of	governmental	funds	is	narrower	than	that	of	government-
wide	financial	statements,	it	is	useful	to	compare	the	information	presented	for	
governmental	funds	with	similar	information	presented	for	governmental	activities	
in	the	government-wide	financial	statements.	By	doing	so,	readers	may	better	
understand	the	long-term	impact	of	the	government’s	near-term	financing	decisions.	
Both	the	governmental	fund	balance	sheet	and	the	governmental	fund	statement	
of	revenues,	expenditures	and	changes	in	fund	balances	provide	a	reconciliation	
to	facilitate	this	comparison	between	governmental	funds	and	governmental	
activities.	These	reconciliations	are	presented	on	Exhibits	C	and	D	of	the	financial	
statements.
The	Town	of	Washington	presents	only	one	column	in	the	governmental	fund	
balance	sheet	and	the	governmental	fund	statement	of	revenues,	expenditures	and	
changes	in	fund	balances.	The	Town’s	major	governmental	fund	is	the	general	
fund.	
The	general	fund	is	the	only	fund	for	which	the	Town	legally	adopted	a	budget.	
The	Budgetary	Comparison	Schedule	-	General	Fund	provides	a	comparison	of	the	
original	and	final	budget	and	the	actual	expenditures	for	the	current	year.

Select Board Summary 2024 Town of Washington 
 
       Clean up and emergency repairs from the December 2023 storm that washed out many roads and 
culverts started the year.  Many of the town roads saw damage, some needing immediate repair to be 
passable.  The assessment was by road commissioner Frank Jones and the Town of Washington EMA 
Director Don Grinnell.  The Washington select board nominated committee members for the Town of 
Washington newly appointed Road Committee. 
 
       The Select Board held its final meeting with the Budget Committee in early February, and again this 
year a concern was the Union Ambulance Service increase.  The Select Board held a joint meeting with 
the Budget Committee and Jesse Thompson from Union Ambulance.  Union Ambulance offered three 
options for the towns to choose from with different levels of staffing.  The select board met with the 
Broadband Committee to begin the planning for a broadband festival in the summer that would start the 
beginning of signing up for the new town broadband utility. 
 
       New hiking trails for the public?  In early March, Peg Hobbs met with the Select Board to discuss 
forming a Town of Washington Trials Committee and investigate the possibility of hiking trails in town for 
the public use as well as fundraising, and research for grants for hiking trails.  The Select Board held a 
public hearing for the Town with Axion, the company partnering with the Town of Washington to install 
and run the town owned broadband utility, Hawkeye Construction, and Maine Connectivity Authority.  
The meeting was to update progress of broadband and discuss the August broadband festival event.  The 
Select Board welcomed the Town’s newly elected select person, Kate Grinnell.  The Select Board would 
like to recognize Tom Johnston for his many contributions over the years as selectman, especially his 
recent effort in negotiating with Axion, and working with the Broadband Committee to precure the 
Federal and State grants of almost three million dollars for the broadband town owned utility.  
 
       In April the select board began making appointments for the many town committees and positions 
for the upcoming year.  Special thanks to all the volunteers for your valuable time and energy to serve on 
these boards and committees.  The Select Board met with the Town attorney to review its options and 
discuss concerns for a moratorium on Multi Family structures.

Greetings	Town	of	Washington,
It	has	been	an	enjoyable	year	for	me	working	in	your	community	and	
every	year	it	is	meeting	residents	that	is	always	a	highlight	for	me.
This	year	was	spent	transferring	deeds,	reviewing	new	construction,	
adjusting	maps,	updating	current	use	programs,	addressing	taxpayers’	
concerns,	and	completing	reports	to	submit	to	Maine	Revenue	Services.	It	
looks	as	though	2024	will	be	much	of	the	same.
Around	April	 st,	I	will	begin	reviewing	building	permits	and	
inspecting	all	new	construction,	so	you	may	see	me	out	and	about	town.	
If	you	plan	to	tear	down	or	remove	any	structures,	please	contact	the	code	
enforcement	officer	to	see	if	a	demo	permit	is	necessary.	It	is	always	a	good	
idea	to	let	the	town	office	know	so	I	am	able	to	remove	these	structures	
from	the	tax	rolls.
If	you	are	not	receiving	the	Homestead	Exemption	check	to	see	if	you	
qualify.	This	is	a	property	tax	exemption	available	for	permanent	residents	
of	Maine	who	have	owned	a	homestead	in	Maine	for	 2-consecutive	
months	prior	to	the	April	st	assessment	closing.	Owners	of	mobile	homes	
also	qualify	for	this	property	tax	exemption.	The	2024	exemption	amount	
will	be	$25,000	of	just	valuation	adjusted	by	the	certified	ratio.	Please	
contact	the	town	office	for	an	application	form,	or	go	to	www.maine.gov/
revenue.	Homestead	Exemption	application	forms	must	be	submitted	to	the	
assessing	office	prior	to	April	st.	
I	also	encourage	people	to	contact	me	if	they	have	questions	about	the	
Veterans	Exemption	or	Tree	Growth,	Farmland,	and	Open	Space	Land	use	
programs.
I	will	be	in	the	town	office	one	day	a	month	to	meet	with	taxpayers	
and	complete	the	work	required	by	the	state.	If	you	have	questions,	please	
call	the	town	office	to	set	up	an	appointment	or	leave	a	message	and	I	will	
contact	you	directly	my	next	day	in	town.
I	am	looking	forward	to	2024	with	you.

Registrar of Voters
There	are	 ,279	registered	voters	in	Washington,	with	 ,2	of	them	
active	as	of	the	end	of	2023.	
Our	town	election	was	March	24,	2023.	Incumbent	Select	Board	member	
Thomas	Johnston	was	unseated	by	Katherine	Grinnell	for	a	three-year	term;	
45	voters	participated	in	the	election.	Running	uncontested,	Noah	Botley	
was	elected	to	a	three-year	term	on	the	RSU	40	Board	of	Directors,	filling	a	
months-long	vacancy	that	previously	was	occupied	by	long-serving	school	
board	member	Guy	Bourrie.	
The	following	day,	March	25,	was	the	annual	town	meeting.	A	total	of	
58	voters	approved	the	town	budget;	approved	the	Town-owned	Property	
Ordinance;	agreed	to	appropriate	$20,000	toward	a	town-wide	revaluation;	
approved	spending	to	upgrade	the	town	website;	insulate	the	town	garage	
ceiling;	 cover	 costs	 for	 excluded	 parcels	 to	 have	 broadband	 internet	
installed;	and	approved	the	purchase	of	an	excavator	for	roads	and	roadside	
maintenance.	
In	June,	there	was	a	Special	Election	to	fill	a	vacancy	in	the	state	House	
of	Representatives	that	pitted	former	Rep.	Abden	Simmons	(R)	against	
challenger	Wendy	Pieh	(D).	Simmons	won	the	race	for	the	District	45	seat	
with	260	votes	to	Pieh’s	94.	The	RSU	40	budget	was	also	validated	in	June	
by	the	455	voters	who	turned	out,	and	the	Union	Ambulance	budget	was	
approved.	
A	special	town	meeting	took	place	July	26	and	22	voters	enacted	a	
Large-scale	Residential	Development	moratorium.	
November	brought	604	voters	to	the	polls	to	decide	on	eight	referendum	
questions	as	well	as	an	$8	million	bond	proposal	to	repair	RSU	40	schools.	
I	am	extremely	grateful	to	our	many	seasoned	-	and	several	newly	minted	
-	election	clerks,	wardens	and	Deputy	Registrars;	without	them	Election	Day	
would	be	tedious	and	daunting.	Each	one	of	you	bring	good	spirits	and	are	
welcoming	faces	to	our	town	voters.	
Anyone	interested	in	becoming	involved	on	Election	Day	is	encouraged	to	
reach	out	to	the	town	office	by	calling	207-845-2897	or	emailing	townclerk@
fairpoint.net.	
Respectfully	submitted,	
STEPHANIE		GRINNELL,
Registrar	of	Voters

Washington Planning Board
The	Washington	Planning	Board	held	public	meetings	the	second	
Tuesday	of	the	month	in	2023,	starting	at	7pm.	The	year	was	full	of	public	
hearings	and	several	approvals.	
It	began	with	an	approval	of	CMP’s	application	to	replace	five	new	
electric	poles	and	upgrade	the	power	lines	linking	the	Cooper	Mills	to	
Highland	substation	transmission	lines.	Scrap	Dog’s	application	was	also	
approved	to	repurpose	the	existing	Mariners	pad	in	the	George	Hall	pit	to	
use	for	composting.	Pike	Industries’	application	for	a	canvas	storage	shed	
to	store	asphalt	shingles	was	approved.	Michael	Johnson’s	application	for	a	
storage	unit	facility	on	the	corner	of	Rt	26	and	Rt	220	was	approved.	Zach	
Ballard	and	Benjamin	Perry	of	Treble	B,	BZB,	applied	for	a	marijuana	
cultivation	greenhouse	at	79	McDowell	Rd;	their	application	did	not	come	
up	for	a	vote	in	2023	because	it	did	not	meet	all	of	the	requirements	in	the	
town	marijuana	ordinance.	Wayne	Kirkpatrick’s	application	was	approved	
for	a	change	of	use	at,	formerly,	Paul’s	Performance	on	Augusta	Road.
Several	other	applications	came	before	the	Planning	Board	which	
did	not	require	Planning	Board	approval	based	on	our	town	ordinances.	
The	year	ended	with	conversations	on	changing	the	definition	of	a	single-
family	home	to	fall	in	line	with	the	State’s	LD	2003	bill	and	to	limit	the	
size	of	multi-unit	properties	allowed	in	the	town.	These	conversations	are	
continuing	into	2024.
Thank	you	to	those	on	the	Board,	to	those	working	in	the	town	office,	
and	to	the	folks	of	our	town.

Emergency Management Agency
The	2023	EMA	report	actually	began	on	December	23,	2022,	as	we	
were	hit	with	a	major	wind	and	rainstorm.	The	storm	caused	downed	trees	
and	wires,	often	blocking	roadways,	along	with	flash	flooding	that	washed	
out	culverts	and	road	shoulders.	Temperatures	were	in	the	50s	during	the	
afternoon	but	began	to	plummet	to	below	freezing	during	the	evening.	Frank	
Jones	and	his	crew	worked	long	into	the	night	clearing	away	woody	debris	
and	making	emergency	repairs	before	the	ground	froze	again.	Our	plan	
was	to	make	the	emergency	repairs	and	make	roads	passable	for	the	winter.	
Final	repairs	would	begin	in	the	spring	of	2023.	In	March,	a	presidential	
disaster	declaration	was	granted	to	several	counties	in	the	state,	including	
Knox	County,	allowing	us	to	apply	for	a	FEMA	Public	Assistance	grant	to	
reimburse	us	for	a	portion	of	the	costs	caused	by	the	storm.
On	April	5th,	Frank	and	I	completed	a	damage	assessment	survey,	and	
he	developed	a	timetable	to	make	permanent	repairs	as	soon	as	the	ground	
was	workable	in	the	spring.	Before	he	could	begin	the	work,	on	May	st,	the	
town	was	hit	with	five	inches	of	rain	in	six	hours.	This	caused	even	more	
damage	than	the	December	storm.	Some	of	the	temporary	repairs	from	the	
December	storm	were	completely	washed	away.	Culverts	were	washed	
downstream	on	Old	County	Road	at	Davis	Stream	and	on	the	far	western	
end	of	Old	County	Road	near	the	Somerville	town	line.	This	caused	those	
two	sites	to	be	closed	for	over	two	months.	Flood	waters	undermined	the	
pavement	at	the	Little	Medomak	culvert	on	Calderwood	Road.	Numerous	
additional	roads	had	washouts	completely	blocking	access	to	roads	and	
driveways	for	a	few	days.	Once	again,	we	applied	for	and	were	granted	
another	presidential	disaster	declaration	in	July.	
The	public	works	crew	spent	most	of	the	summer	making	storm	damage	
repairs	instead	of	their	usual	summer	work.	The	work	for	the	repairs	resulting	
from	the	December	2022	storm	totaled	$50,684.	FEMA	has	approved	the	
documented	expenditures,	and	will	be	reimbursing	the	town	75%	of	the	
disaster	costs	($38,03)	and	the	State	of	Maine	will	eventually	reimburse	
the	town	and	additional	5%	of	the	costs	($7,602)	leaving	the	town	to	fund	
the	remaining	0%.
Repairs	have	been	completed	for	most	of	the	May	2023	storm	damages.

is	now	complete,	except	for	small	culvert	projects	on	Mountain	Road	and

Washington Lakes Water Quality
This	is	the	45th	year	of	recording	water	quality	data,	and	the	ninth	
annual	summary	report	on	lake	water	quality,	supported	by	Washington	
Lakes	Watershed	Association.	We	regularly	monitor	water	clarity	(to	
determine	algae	level)	and	dissolved	oxygen	and	temperature	profiles.	
Annually	we	collect	samples	for	a	comprehensive	chemistry	analysis.	All	
of	this	data	is	certified	and	then	becomes	part	of	the	Maine	DEP	database.	
The	complete	report	can	be	found	at	Gibbs	Library	or	on	the	web	at	
Washingtonlakesassociation.org	using	the	About	Us	tab.
2023	was	WET.	Temperature	and	precipitation	have	big	impacts	on	
water	quality.	The	rainfall	amounts	in	March	through	September	were	
consistently	at	the	maximum	in	monthly	NOAA	records.	We	ended	this	
seven-month	period	with	6”	more	rain	than	normal.	As	a	result,	there	was	
a	dramatic	increase	runoff	into	lakes	and	lake	levels	stayed	at	historical	
highs	all	summer.	Temperatures	for	the	same	period	were	somewhat	higher	
than	normal,	with	early	April,	May,	and	early	September	having	high	peaks,	
while	June,	July	and	August	were	quite	normal	with	the	exception	of	a	cold	
period	in	early	June.
For	Washington	Pond,	Secchi	Disk	readings	(transparency)	were	
consistently	at	the	high	(good)	range	of	historical	data.	Dissolved	Oxygen	
tests	were	within	normal	range.	Chlorophyll	and	phosphorus	(proxies	for	
algae	growth)	were	high	(not	good)	but	not	unexpected	with	warmer	water	
(surface	temps	peaked	early	in	July	and	stayed	warm	all	summer).	Higher	
runoff	contributes	phosphorus	pollution	to	these	elevations	also.
Crystal	Lake	has	always	behaved	differently:	Secchi	was	lower	early	to	
mid-summer	but	regained	high	readings	late	August.	Chlorophyll	was	high	
(as	it	was	in	Washington	Pond)	but	phosphorus	was	right	on	normal.
There	were	no	invasive	plant	species	in	either	lake	in	2023	to	our	
knowledge.	Although	we	do	not	think	we	have	any	invasive	plants,	we	
cannot	be	sure	without	looking	at	high	probability	sites.	This	is	a	major	
weakness	in	our	“preventative”	activities	and	we	urgently	need	volunteers	
to	take	on	this	important	task.	Training	in	this	valuable	work	is	available	
from	Lake	Stewards	of	Maine.

LifeFlight of Maine
Ten Facts and Frequently Asked Questions about LifeFlight
	
1. LifeFlight is a private non-profit charitable organization with 
a public mission serving all of Maine. 	We	care	for	all	patients	
regardless	of	insurance	status	or	ability	to	pay	for	care	with	helicopters	
based	in	Bangor,	Lewiston,	and	Sanford,	a	fixed-wing	airplane	based	in	
Bangor,	rapid	response	vehicles,	and	specialized	ground	ambulances.	
In	FY2023	LifeFlight	provided	$.7	million	of	care	to	patients	without	
insurance	or	the	means	to	cover	the	cost	of	care,	as	well	as	significant	
discounts	for	Medicare	and	MaineCare	patients.
2. In FY23, 2,468 patients were transported from 124 Maine 
communities,	islands,	and	unorganized	townships	-	about	 	patient	
every	3.5	hours.	LifeFlight	has	cared	for	more	than	37,000	patients	
since	September	998.
3. What is the Community Giving Campaign? 	Each	year	in	the	Fall,	
LifeFlight	reaches	out	to	every	community	in	the	state	to	ask	for	support.	
The	request	includes	a	suggested	donation	based	on	population;	a	$.00	
per	capita	rate	for	towns	that	have	up	to	,000	residents;	a	$0.50	per	
capita	rate	for	those	with	up	to	2,000	residents;	and	a	$0.25	per	capita	
rate	for	all	others,	with	asks	typically	capped	at	$2,000.	The	average	
gift	size	in	2023	was	$596,	and	some	towns	give	generously	beyond	
what	they	are	asked.
4. How is LifeFlight funded? LifeFlight	operates	much	like	a	hospital	
emergency	department,	taking	care	of	all	patients	and	billing	insurance	
companies.	 We	contract	 with	 most	 major	 commercial	 insurance	
companies	and	participate	in	the	Medicare	and	Medicaid	programs.	
Most	of	our	operational	expenses	are	covered	by	patient	fees,	but	
we	rely	on	the	LifeFlight	Foundation	to	support	the	purchase	of	new	
aircraft,	 medical	 equipment,	 infrastructure	 improvements,	 and	 to	
provide	clinical	education	around	the	state.
5. LifeFlight helicopters, the airplane, and specialty ground vehicles

Washington Broadband Committee
The	Washington	Broadband	Committee	(WBBC)	was	formed	in	August	
2020	to	explore	affordable	options	for	high-speed	internet	that	everyone	
in	the	town	had	access	to.	After	much	research	and	meetings	with	several	
potential	partners,	residents	of	Washington	voted	in	August	202	to	partner	
with	Axiom	for	a	plan	to	find	a	better	option	than	our	incumbent	Internet	
Service	Provider	(ISP).	In	February	2022	Axiom	Technologies	received	
federal	(NTIA)	and	state	grant	(CONNECTME	Authority)	funding	to	
partner	with	Washington	to	build	a	municipally	owned	fiber	optic	system.	
Washington	owns	the	infrastructure	and	Axiom	is	our	partnering	provider.	
Having	a	municipally	owned	system	is	the	only	way	that	rural	communities	
can	assure	that	there	will	be	equal	access	to	high-speed	internet	to	all.	
Consolidated	Communications,	Inc.	(CCI)	is	Washington’s	primary	and	
incumbent	provider.	CCI	is	our	Incumbent	Local	Exchange	Carrier	(ILEC).	
They	provide	telephone	service	and	basic	Digital	Subscriber	Line/Loop	
(DSL)	internet	over	their	copper	telephone	landlines.	They	legally	fulfill	
the	State	of	Maine’s	PUC	requirement	of	a	Provider	of	Last	Resort	(POLR).	
They	supply	(most	of)	us	basic	service	and	are	not	required	to	upgrade	their	
system	to	provide	us	better	service.	
Some	of	you	may	have	subscribed	to	some	other	ISPs	in	town	and	have	
been	very	happy.	Not	everyone	in	town	is	eligible	for	Starlink,	for	example,	
and	many	cannot	afford	the	cost.	The	goal	of	the	WBBC	and	the	Town	of	
Washington	was	to	work	with	a	new	Competitive	Local	Exchange	Carrier	
(CLEC)	that	could	offer	affordable	fiber	optic	internet	to	everyone	in	town	
that	would	like	access	to	faster	and	more	reliable	service.	We	found	that	
with	Axiom!	At	the	Annual	Town	Meeting	in	2022	we	voted	to	partner	with	
them	to	build	a	new	fiber	optic	system.	
The	process	of	building	our	fiber	optic	system	has	been	happening	
throughout	the	year,	mostly	behind	the	scenes.	Building	brand	new	
infrastructure	in	our	town	has	many	steps	and	has	to	be	coordinated	by	
many	different	entities.	This	spring,	we	should	start	to	see	more	visible	
action	in	town	as	the	fiber	infrastructure	installations	begin.
We	are	fortunate	that	we	have	two	different	organizations	managing	
our	project	and	communicating	with	the	Federal	government	to	draw	down	
funding	for	the	project.	The	Maine	Connectivity	Authority	and	Tilson	
Technology,	a	firm	hired	by	the	state,	have	been	very	busy	making	sure	that	
we	make	deadlines	and	that	the	project	moves	forward	to	completion.

In	January,	I	was	privileged	to	take	the	Oath	of	Office	to	begin	my

health	care,	leading	to	the	largest	decline	in	the	uninsured	rate	of	any	state

diversifying	our	economy.	In	2022,	our	state’s	gross	domestic	product	–	a	
key	measure	of	economic	growth	–	grew	at	the	9th	fastest	rate	in	the	United	
States.	People	are	moving	to	Maine	at	a	rate	higher	than	any	other	New	
England	state,	and	at	one	of	the	highest	rates	in	the	nation.	We	have	enacted	
balanced	budgets,	and	we	have	built	up	Maine’s	“Rainy	Day”	fund	to	a

NOTE	A	-	SUMMARY	OF	SIGNIFICANT	ACCOUNTING	POLICIES:
The	accounting	policies	of	the	Town	of	Washington	conform	to	generally	
accepted	accounting	principles	as	applicable	to	governmental	units.
.	Financial	Reporting	Entity
	 	 The	Town	of	Washington	was	incorporated	in	8.	The	Town	operates	under	
a	town	meeting	form	of	government.
	In	evaluating	the	Town	of	Washington	as	a	reporting	entity,	management	
has	addressed	all	potential	component	units.	The	primary	criteria	for	including	a	
component	reporting	entity	are	the	exercise	of	financial	accountability	by	the	Town	
of	Washington’s	municipal	officials.
	 	 The	Town’s	financial	statements	are	prepared	in	accordance	with	accounting	
principles	generally	accepted	in	the	United	States	of	America.	The	Governmental	
Accounting	Standards	Board	(GASB)	is	responsible	for	establishing	Generally	
Accepted	Accounting	Principles	(GAAP)	for	state	and	local	governments	through	
its	pronouncements	(Statements	and	Interpretations).	Governments	are	also	required	
to	follow	the	pronouncements	of	the	Financial	Accounting	Standards	Board	(FASB)	
issued	through	November	30,	989	(when	applicable)	that	do	not	conflict	with	or	
contradict	GASB	pronouncements.	
2.	Basic	Financial	Statements	-	Government-Wide	Statements
	 	 The	Town’s	basic	 financial	 statements	 include	 both	 government-wide	
(reporting	the	Town	as	a	whole)	and	fund	financial	statements	(reporting	the	Town’s	
major	funds).	Both	the	government-wide	and	fund	financial	statements	categorize	
primary	 activities	 as	 governmental.	 The	 Town’s	fire	 protection,	 recreation,	
public	works,	and	general	administrative	services	are	classified	as	governmental	
activities.
	 	 In	the	government-wide	Statements	of	Net	Position,	the	governmental	column	
is	presented	on	a	consolidated	basis	by	column,	and	is	reported	on	a	full	accrual,	
economic	resource	basis,	which	recognizes	all	long-term	assets	and	receivables	as	
well	as	long-term	debt	and	obligations.	The	Town’s	net	position	is	reported	in	three	
parts	-	net	invested	in	capital	assets;	restricted;	and	unrestricted.	The	Town	first	
utilizes	restricted	resources	to	finance	qualifying	activities.
	 	 The	government-wide	Statements	of	Activities	reports	both	the	gross	and	
net	cost	of	each	of	the	Town’s	functions	(fire,	public	works,	administrative,	etc.).	
The	functions	are	also	supported	by	general	government	revenues	(property,	certain	
intergovernmental	revenues,	fines,	permits,	and	charges,	etc.).	The	Statements	of	
Activities	reduces	gross	expenses	(including	depreciation)	by	related	program	
revenues,	and	operating	and	capital	grants.	Program	revenues	must	be	directly	
associated	with	the	function	(fire,	public	works,	etc.).	Operating	grants	include	
operating-specific	and	discretionary	(either	operating	or	capital)	grants	while	the	
capital	grants	column	reflects	capital-specific	grants.
	 	 The	net	costs	(by	function)	are	normally	covered	by	general	revenue	(property,	
intergovernmental	revenues,	interest	income,	etc.).

	 	 This	government-wide	focus	is	more	on	the	sustainability	of	the	Town	as	an	
entity	and	the	change	in	the	Town’s	net	position	resulting	from	the	current	year’s	
activities.
3.	Basic	Financial	Statements	-	Fund	Financial	Statements
	 	 The	financial	transactions	of	the	Town	are	reported	in	individual	funds	in	the	
fund	financial	statements.	Each	fund	is	accounted	for	by	providing	a	separate	set	
of	self-balancing	accounts	that	comprise	its	assets,	liabilities,	reserves,	fund	equity,	
revenues,	and	expenditures/expenses.	The	various	funds	are	reported	by	generic	
classification	within	the	financial	statements.
	 	 The	following	fund	types	are	used	by	the	Town:
	 	 	 a.	 Governmental	Funds:
	 	 	 	 	 The	focus	of	the	governmental	funds’	measurement	(in	the	fund	
statements)	is	upon	determination	of	financial	position	and	changes	in	financial	
position	(sources,	uses,	and	balances	of	financial	resources)	rather	than	upon	net	
income.	The	following	is	a	description	of	the	governmental	fund	of	the	Town:
	 	 	 	 	.	 General	Fund:
	 	 	 	 	 	 	 General	Fund	is	the	general	operating	fund	of	the	Town.	It	is	used	
to	account	for	all	financial	resources	except	those	required	to	be	accounted	for	in	
another	fund.
	 	 Additionally,	the	Town	reports	the	following	fund	type:
	 	 	 	 	 Fiduciary	Funds:
	 	 	 	 	 Fiduciary	Funds	are	used	to	report	assets	held	in	a	trustee	or	agency	
capacity	for	others	and	therefore	are	not	available	to	support	Town	programs.	The	
reporting	focus	is	on	net	assets	and	changes	in	net	assets	and	is	reported	using	
accounting	principles	similar	to	proprietary	funds.
	 	 	 	 	 The	emphasis	in	fund	financial	statements	is	on	the	major	funds	in	
either	the	governmental	or	business-type	activities	categories.	Nonmajor	funds	by	
category	are	summarized	into	a	single	column.	GASB	No.	34	sets	forth	minimum	
criteria	(percentage	of	the	assets,	liabilities,	revenues,	or	expenditures/expenses	of	
either	fund	category)	for	the	determination	of	major	funds.
	 	 	 	 	 The	Town’s	fiduciary	funds	are	presented	in	the	fiduciary	fund	financial	
statements.	Since	by	definition	these	assets	are	being	held	for	the	benefit	of	a	third	
party	and	cannot	be	used	to	address	activities	or	obligations	of	the	government,	
these	funds	are	not	incorporated	into	the	government-wide	statements.
4.	Basis	of	Accounting
	 	 Basis	of	accounting	refers	to	the	point	at	which	revenues	or	expenditures/
expenses	are	recognized	in	the	accounts	and	reported	in	the	financial	statements.	It	
relates	to	the	timing	of	the	measurements	made	regardless	of	the	measurement	focus	
applied:
	 	 	 a.	 Accrual:
	 	 	 	 	 Governmental	activities	in	the	government-wide	financial	statements	and	
fiduciary	fund	financial	statements	are	presented	on	the	accrual	basis	of	accounting.	
Revenues	are	recognized	when	earned	and	expenses	are	recognized	when	incurred.

	 	 	 b.	 Modified	Accrual:
	 	 	 	 	 The	governmental	funds	financial	statements	are	presented	on	the	
modified	 accrual	 basis	 of	 accounting.	 Under	 the	 modified	 accrual	 basis	 of	
accounting,	revenues	are	recorded	when	susceptible	to	accrual;	i.e.	both	measurable	
and	available.	“Available”	means	collectible	within	the	current	period	or	within	
60	days	after	year	end.	Expenditures	are	generally	recognized	under	the	modified	
accrual	basis	of	accounting	when	the	related	liability	is	incurred.	The	exception	to	
this	general	rule	is	that	principal	and	interest	on	general	obligation	long-term	debt,	
if	any,	is	recognized	when	due.
5.	Financial	Statement	Amounts
	 	 a.	 Cash	and	Cash	Equivalents:
	 	 	 	 The	Town	has	defined	cash	and	cash	equivalents	to	include	cash	on	hand,	
demand	deposits,	and	cash	with	fiscal	agents.	Statutes	authorize	the	Treasurer	of	the	
Town,	as	directed	by	the	municipal	officers,	to	invest	all	municipal	funds,	including	
reserve	and	trust	funds,	to	the	extent	that	the	terms	of	the	instrument,	order,	or	article	
creating	the	fund	do	not	prohibit	the	investment	in	financial	institutions	as	described	
in	Section	5706	MRSA	and	securities	as	described	in	Sections	57	through	577	
MRSA.
	 	 b.	 Accounts	Receivable:

ARTICLE I. Purpose and Authority
This	Ordinance	is	enacted	pursuant	to	authority	granted	under	30-A	M.R.S.	§	
300.	The	purpose	of	this	Ordinance	is	to	set	forth	procedures	and	standards	
for	the	use	of	Town	Designated	Public	Trails	on	Private	or	Town	Owned	
Property	in	the	Town	of	Washington	Maine.
ARTICLE II. Definitions
Private	Property:	Real	Estate	titled	to	a	party	other	than	the	Town	or	
Inhabitants	of	Washington	as	recorded	in	the	Registry	of	Deeds.
Town	Designated	Trail:	A	trail	or	system	of	trails	designated	by	the	
Washington	Select	Board	as	a	“Public	Trail”.
Town	Owned	Property:	Real	Estate	titled	to	the	Town	or	Inhabitants	of	
Washington	as	recorded	in	the	Registry	of	Deeds	or	foreclosed	through	the	
statutory	tax	foreclosure	process.
ARTICLE III. Select Board Authority
The	Select	Board	may	designate	Public	Trails	on	any	Town	Owned	
Property	in	the	Town	of	Washington.	The	Select	Board	may	also	enter	into	
an	agreement	with	the	owner	of	Private	Property	in	Washington	for	the	use	
of	that	property	to	include	a	Public	Trail	pursuant	to	this	ordinance.
After	the	mapping	and	marking	of	the	Public	Trail,	the	Select	Board	shall	
take	a	second	vote	opening	the	trail	to	Public	Use.	If	this	second	vote	fails,	
the	Public	Trail	shall	not	be	opened	to	Public	Use	unless	and	until	another	
majority	vote	of	the	Select	Board	opening	the	Public	Trail.
ARTICLE IV . Public Trails on Public Property
Public	Trails	on	Public	Property	shall	be	designated	by	a	vote	of	the	Select	
Board	after	a	hearing	with	notice	posted	for	at	least	4	days	in	the	Town	
Office.	The	Select	Board	may	also	discontinue	the	same	said	Public	Trails	
by	a	vote	with	notice	posted	for	at	least	4	days	in	the	Town	Office.	The	
Town	shall	retain	an	easement	over	any	Public	Trail	over	which	the	Town	
acquired	an	easement	appurtenant.

ARTICLE V . Agreements for Public Trails on Private Property
When	a	majority	of	the	Select	Board	wishes	to	obtain	a	Trail	Agreement	
with	the	Private	Land	Owner,	the	Board	shall	vote	to	open	discussions	with	
a	Private	Land	Owner	to	attempt	to	establish	a	Public	Trail	by	easement	or	
license	across	the	said	private	property.
The	Agreement	must	set	out	the	following	minimum	terms:
.	 The	 Public	Trail	shall	be	marked	at	least	every	00’	or	closer	as	
necessary	so	that	each	marker	shall	be	in	clear	view	of	a	marker	
or	flag	further	down	the	trail	and	further	up	the	trail	indicating	the	
path	that	the	public	is	to	follow	while	on	the	Private	Property.
2.	 Any	Public	Trail	shall	specify	a	license	or	easement	to	the	public	
to	pass	and	repass	on	all	land	within	a	minimum	of	25’	wide	strip	
of	land	as	agreed	by	the	Private	Landowner	and	the	Town.
3.	 That	the	Town	shall	indemnify	the	Private	Land	Owner	from	any	
claims	or	damages	and	shall	defend	the	Private	Landowner	from	
suits	by	members	of	the	Public	traversing	the	Public	Trail.	
4.	 The	Town	shall	notify	its	liability	insurance	carrier	in	advance	of	
opening	the	new	Public	Trail	to	use	to	determine	the	insurance	
cost	to	the	Town	by	providing	a	Public	Trail	across	private	land.	
The	Town	shall	place	this	ordinance	with	the	insurance	carrier	to	
notify	the	carrier	of	the	indemnification	provisions	required	by	the	
ordinance	to	accurately	calculate	the	insurance	cost	for	the	new	
Public	Trail.
5.	 The	Town	and	Private	Landowner	must	agree	on	the	signage	and	
trail	marking	method	to	be	used	on	the	private	property.
6.	 The	Town	and	the	Private	Landowner	shall	agree	on	a	permanent	
easement,	an	easement	for	years,	or	on	a	license	for	the	new	Public
