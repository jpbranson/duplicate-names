In 2018, the LSU AgCenter entered into a 5-year cooperative agreement with the
Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) to address the nutrition and
physical activity environments in six rural Louisiana parishes with some of the
highest obesity rates in the state (Assumption, St. Helena, Tensas, Madison,
Morehouse, and East Carroll). Through the LSU AgCenter's Cooperative Extension
Healthy Communities initiative, multi-level, community-driven approaches are
utilized to identify and implement Policy, Systems, and Environmental (PSE) change
strategies, with the goal of promoting healthy behaviors among residents and
improving obesity rates and quality of life. 
Healthy communities are places where people come together to make their
communities better for everyone through collaboration, inclusion, community
ownership, and a long-term commitment to healthy lifestyles. The mission of
Madison Parish Healthy Communities is to improve 
the health and well-being of Madison Parish 
residents through education, community outreach 
programs, partnerships and collaborations, and 
engagement in physical activity.

resources, stakeholders, and community assets

13 food outlets across 6 rural parishes implemented healthy retail changes reaching an
estimated 34,289 residents.
7 food pantries increased capacity to procure healthier foods by installing new refrigeration
and storage. 
4 food pantries adopted policies to improve the healthfulness of donated and procured foods.
2 new farmers' markets were established, and 4 existing farmers' markets were enhanced by
implementing SNAP acceptance and connecting produce vendors to local markets. 
24 new connections made between local producers and food retail opportunities as a result
of farmers’ market efforts. 
10 destinations and 8 linear miles have been enhanced through policies and/or plans,
potentially impacting 12,414 rural residents. From these plans, 2 linear miles of activity-friendly
routes have been improved through the installation of wayfinding signs, speed bumps,
crosswalks, and sidewalks.
3 connectivity plans have been passed to address 6 linear miles of activity-friendly routes,
which include enhancements like bike repair stations, playground equipment, and recreation
amenities (e.g., benches, lighting, trash cans, and signage) at various community sites (e.g.,
libraries, parks, schools, and levees). Collaboration with community stakeholders has also
resulted in policies that seek to leverage existing community assets to create more
opportunities for physical activity.  
A joint-use policy was passed in St. Helena that allows community members to use an existing
school track for physical activity outside of school hours.
