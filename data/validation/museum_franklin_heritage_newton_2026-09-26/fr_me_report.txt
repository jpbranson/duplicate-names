In Dedication 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Robert W Fernald 
1945 - 2018 
 
Robert W Fernald, the last Fernald Town Clerk of Franklin, will 
always be remembered.  The line of Fernald Town Clerks started in 
1943 in the house on Hog Bay Road with Bob’s father Lloyd. Then 
his mother, Dorothy followed in 1980.  The reins were handed over 
to Bob in 1993.  Bob was more than the Town Clerk of Franklin.  
He was an educator, a historian, a foodie before there were foodies, 
a collector of items – some valuable, some not.  Bob was a giver of 
his services, his knowledge, his kitchen, and a shoulder when 
needed.  He was truly a historian of Franklin and its people.  When 
anybody had a question about who was who, or where was what, 
Bob knew!

Dear Friends, 
It is an honor to represent Maine in the United States Senate.  I am 
grateful for the trust the people of our state have placed in me and 
welcome this opportunity to share some key accomplishments from 
this past year.  
As Chairman of the Senate Aging Committee, I worked to help 
ensure the well-being of our seniors.  The Senior$afe Act I authored 
became law last year and is empowering banks, credit unions, and 
other financial institutions to better protect seniors from financial 
fraud.  
 
Following extensive committee investigations of prescription drug 
pricing, additional legislation I crafted became law, ending the 
egregious practice of pharmacy “gag clauses” that prevented 
pharmacists from informing patients on how to pay the lowest 
possible price. 
 
This year, I was also successful in securing an extra $425 million 
for Alzheimer’s research—the largest funding increase ever—
bringing the total to $2.34 billion.  Additionally, the 
bipartisan BOLD Act I authored will create public health 
infrastructure to combat Alzheimer’s by promoting education, early 
diagnosis, and improved care management.  
 
More than 40 million Americans—including 178,000 Mainers—are 
caregivers for parents, spouses, children, and other loved ones with 
disabilities or illnesses, such as Alzheimer’s.  The RAISE Family 
Caregivers Act I authored was signed into law last year, giving 
caregivers more resources and training to better balance the full-
time job of caregiving. Another law I wrote will help grandparents 
who are raising grandchildren, largely due to the opioid addiction 
crisis. 
 


PAGE 13
~ 12 ~ 
In addition to helping seniors, a major accomplishment over the 
past year is the increased federal investment in biomedical research 
that is leading to progress in the fight against numerous devastating 
diseases. Congress has boosted funding for the National Institutes 
of Health by $7 billion in just the last three years, bringing total 
funding to more than $39 billion. 
  
One of my highest priorities as Chairman of the Transportation 
Appropriations Subcommittee is to improve our nation’s crumbling 
infrastructure and ensure that Maine’s needs are addressed.  Since 
the Better Utilizing Investments to Leverage Development 
(BUILD) Transportation Grants program, formerly known as 
TIGER, was established in 2009, I have secured $160 million for 
vital transportation projects throughout Maine. 
 
Congress also delivered a Farm Bill last year, which includes many 
important provisions that will help the agriculture industry in Maine 
and across the country.  Specifically, I secured provisions that will 
strengthen support for young farmers, improve local farm-to-market 
efforts, and increase funding for organic research. 
  
Congress took decisive action to address the opioid addiction 
epidemic. In addition to appropriating $8.5 billion in federal 
funding last year, Congress enacted the SUPPORT for Patients and 
Communities Act, a comprehensive package that embraces the 
multipronged approach I have long advocated for this epidemic: 
prevention, treatment, recovery, and enforcement to stop drug 
trafficking.  
  
Maine plays a key role in ensuring a strong national defense.  In 
2018, Congress provided funding for five ships to be built at Bath 
Iron Works, which will help to keep our nation safe and provide our 
skilled shipbuilders a steady job.  I also secured more than $162 
million for infrastructure projects at Portsmouth Naval Shipyard to 
support their important work to overhaul Navy submarines.

I appreciate the opportunity to serve Maine in the United States

May 2019 be a good year for you, your family, your community,

January 3, 2019 
 
 
Dear Friends, 
  
 
As I travel Maine, I hear from people who live in every corner of our 
state. I hear about their achievements, their successes, their work to 
improve their communities – I hear about the hope they have for our 
state. I also hear about our challenges, and all the work we have left to 
do.  As I see it, that’s my job: to listen to you, act where I can to build 
on what’s good, and work on the tough parts.  As 2018 comes to a 
close, I wanted to take a moment to share an update on some of the 
work we’re doing in Washington to lift up the accomplishments of 
Maine people and make progress on the challenges they face. 
 
From Portland to Presque Isle, from Milo to Camden, I hear about the 
pain that the opioid epidemic is inflicting on Maine communities.  I’ve 
met with Maine people in recovery, family members of those 
struggling with substance use disorders, treatment providers, and law 
enforcement officials to learn about their experiences with this terrible 
disease, and everyone agrees that in order to fully respond to these 
problems, we need a stronger federal effort to end the opioid epidemic.  
Fortunately, some help is on the way – in October, we overwhelmingly 
passed a sweeping, bipartisan opioids bill. I’ve pushed hard for this 
type of legislation and was proud to have provisions I’ve advocated for 
included in the bill.  These priorities have been guided by the voices of 
Maine people, and we’ll keep working to confront this tragic problem.   
  
I’ve also worked to strengthen the future of our forest economy. 
Maine’s forests have powered our state’s economy for generations, 
especially in our rural communities.  So, when rapid shifts in the 
market led to the closure of many pulp and paper mills and biomass 
power plants, it required a collaborative approach to support future 
growth in this important industry.  That’s why, together with the other 
members of the state’s Congressional delegation, I pushed to establish 
the Economic Development Assessment Team (EDAT).  This 
integrated, multiagency effort aims to foster innovation and 
commercialization in Maine’s forest economy, and we’re already 
seeing the benefits: in recent months, several forest industry businesses 
have announced significant investments into Maine operations, and in 
September 2018, the Forest Opportunity Roadmap (FOR)/Maine 

PAGE 16
~ 15 ~ 
released an action plan to make sure this industry, and the rural 
communities it supports, can continue to thrive for generations to 
come. 
 
As I close this letter, please allow me to express my gratitude to each 
of you – for your dedication to our state, and to one another.  It’s often 
said that Maine is like a big small town (with very long streets) —
 that’s because at our heart, we’re one big community.  It’s not only a 
pleasure to serve you –   it’s a pleasure to know you.  Thank you for 
being the reason Maine is so special.  Mary and I hope that 2019 will 
be a good year for you, your family, your community, and our great 
State. 
 
 
Best, 
 
 
 
 
Angus S. King 
United States Senator

Select Board Report 
 
Greetings to fellow citizens of the Town of Franklin –  
 
This past year has been a relatively quiet year for the town.  The 
office staff said good-bye to Melissa Burkhart and her new little 
daughter.  We thank Melissa for her service as Assistant Town 
Administrator and Treasurer.  In April, we welcomed Aleta Fusco 
to our office staff.  As Elaine Brown was out for the month of April, 
Aleta received her training by fire with Sharon Blakely.  She 
learned about vehicle registrations, dog licenses, and tax payments, 
along with starting to learn about Franklin.   
 
The office is open from 7:30 am to 5:00 pm – Monday through 
Friday, except for Holidays and once in a while lunch time. 
 
Each year we are upgrading some of the computer equipment.  A 
server and better battery back-ups were installed this year.  During 
the next year, the assessor’s computer is targeted for an upgrade. 
 
Any person that has come into the office or the Meeting Room may 
have noticed the change in lightings.  The Trustees replaced all of 
the old fluorescent lights to the better LED.  These new lights have 
a softer glow and are much easier on the eyes. 
 
We resurfaced the Tracy Road and finished paving the Alder Brook 
Hill Road.  Before resurfacing a road, any culverts that need 
replacing are done and ditches are cleaned out.  Most of the ditching 
on the Donnells Pond Road is complete and are hoping to resurface 
it in the next couple of years.  As always, Martins Ridge Road and 
South Bay Road are graded each year as soon as the town roads are 
not posted in the spring. 
 
Georges Pond Road – the shoulders were completed this year.  The 
public boat ramp was installed beside the old boat ramp, as we 
discovered the town does not own the old boat ramp property.  A 
few more concrete planks were acquired for the ramp to help keep 
boaters off the beach area.  They will be installed sometime this 
coming summer. 

PAGE 22
~ 21 ~ 
 
 
Trash and recycling.  Not always a pleasant subject.  The new plant 
that will handle our municipal solid waste should be in use before 
the summer.  For most of us, that does not mean any change in how 
we handle our trash.  We have invited MRC to attend Town 
Meeting to answer any questions you may have about trash and 
recycling in the future.  MRC and Fiberight (now Coastal 
Resources) should not to be confused with Coastal Recycling.  
 
However, Coastal Recycling does not have a bright future.  If we 
vote ‘Yes’ to dissolve Coastal Recycling along with other member 
towns, then Coastal Recycling will close in April.  Any equipment 
will be sold and the funds distributed back to the member towns.  
The Select Board of Franklin, along with Sullivan, Sorrento, 
Hancock, and Winter Harbor boards independently did agree to 
dissolve Coastal Recycling. 
 
The Franklin Water Department has seen a changing of the guard, 
as Peter Brady retired in December.  Thank you Pete for your years 
of service to Franklin.  Dana Smith who has been learning under 
Pete is stepping up to make sure the water continues to flows. 
 
There have been several donations of services this year that need to 
be recognized.  Frank for a buffing of the floors, Richard for gray 
stones at the monument, Joel for the installation of the ice rink – 
just to name a few.  There are countless others that help, donate 
time and services, work freely, and give of themselves to make 
Franklin a place to call home. 
 
 
Respectfully submitted, 
 
 
Dawn R Carter 
Jeffrey Albee

Notice to Taxpayers 
 
State Law requires that the residents and non-residents of a 
town must present to the Tax Assessors of the town a true list of 
Real Estate and Personal Property they possess on the first day of 
April each year.  Standardized forms are available at the Assessors 
Office and should be filled out and returned to the Assessors. 
Homestead Exemption Applications, Veterans Exemption 
Applications, Tree Growth Applications, Open Space and Farm 
Land Applications, Working Waterfront Applications, BETA and 
BETE applications are available at the Assessors Office and may be 
downloaded from the Franklin web-site.  The Franklin website

2018 ANNUAL REPORT 
 
There is no easy way to present this so I’ll opt for the straight 
forward approach.  The Coastal Recycling Board of Directors on 
December 12, 2018 voted unanimously to recommend to their five 
member towns that Coastal Recycling be dissolved.  
 
As you may have heard over the past couple of years, recycling is 
undergoing considerable changes.  None of these changes seem to 
encourage or promote recycling.  The overseas (especially China) 
markets for recyclables have disappeared.  We theorize this 
disappearance has resulted in a glut of products in the recyclables 
market thereby resulting in marked decline in the prices paid to 
recycling organizations such as Coastal.  As an example, the price 
paid for cardboard has gone from as high as $180 per ton to the 
current price of $50 per ton.   In addition to the very low price per 
ton, we have to pay the freight costs. Processors no longer pay for 
products like paperboard (cereal boxes, junk mail, office paper etc.) 
and as a matter of fact, they charge us for the products we ship and 
again, we must pay the freight to ship these items.  On a small 
budget such as ours, we can’t absorb these costs therefore is we 
have discontinued recycling paperboard products. 
 
By March 31, 2019 the five towns making up Coastal Recycling are 
required to vote on the dissolution of Coastal.  Approval of the 
Board’s recommendation requires a 2/3rd’ majority vote by the 
member Towns.  In other words, if more than two towns vote no on 
dissolution, the recommendation to dissolve is defeated.  If the 


PAGE 32
~ 31 ~ 
towns vote to support the recommendation to dissolve, Coastal will 
be closing down operations at the end of the day on April 27, 2019.  
No recyclables will be accepted after that.  
 
Should the towns vote to continue the Coastal operation, the board 
has prepared a budget covering the July 1, 2019 to June 30, 2020 
time frame.  This budget calls for total operating costs of 
$111,350.00 compared with operating costs for the prior year of 
$117,550.00.  This represents a decrease of 5.3%. 
 
The Town’s share of this proposal (25.2%) will increase from 
$10,913 to $18,195.  This represents an increase of 66.73%.

Frenchman’s Bay Regional Shellfish 
Conservation Ordinance 
 
Mission Statement:  “The town of Ellsworth, Franklin, Hancock, 
Lamoine, Sorrento, Sullivan, and Trenton will act collaboratively as 
stewards to preserve, protect, manage, and enhance the shellfish 
resources and ecological well-being of the Frenchman’s Bay 
Region and to ensure a sustainable harvest of shellfish and 
opportunity for those who make their living on the tide.” 
 
 Our program is entering into its tenth (10th) year.  In most areas, 
the program has been a great success.  Digging area rotations and 
flat closures have ensured harvesters have areas to dig throughout 
the year.  Seeding programs are done throughout the year by 
commercial license holders on the committee. 
 
 Most importantly, the ordinance protects the seven (7) town 
area from harvesters coming in from other areas in the State. 
 
 Currently, there are seventy (70) commercial harvesters from 
the seven (7) town region.  Six (6) of those harvesters reside in the 
Town of Franklin.  There were also over two hundred (200+) 
recreational licenses issued.  Funds generated from license sales and 
town contributions assist with enforcement of the ordinance. 
 
 All commercial harvesters are required to do twelve (12) hours 
of service, per year, in order to be able to have their license 
renewed.  This service includes shore clean-up in all conservation 
towns, seeding, and surveys. 
 
 Commerical harvesters now have the option of opting out of 
their conservation hours.  If they do so, they would be required to 
pay more money for their commerical license depending on how 
many hours they work. 
 
 With input from our havesters our committtees work hard to 
ensure this program continues to be successful.

  Donated objects, once of significant value to

 Assisting anyone who seeks help researching a family tree

Franklin Library 
 
For the Franklin Library, 2018 was a year of important 
developments.  As winter ended, we marked the first time in its 
history that the Library had been open year-round.  In its many 
decades as a meeting place for the Franklin’s Women’s Club, the 
library building had always been closed by October.  In 2017, with 
the help of a Stephen & Tabitha King Foundation grant, the 
building had been insulated, heating and electrical systems updated, 
and the windows repaired or reinforced.  With these improvements 
and the addition of rebuilt stairs, a new handicapped ramp and a 
beautiful deck, we were able to remain open and continue our 
programs throughout the cold months and into spring 2018. 
 
In March, after many years of hard work to make improvements in 
both the quality and organization our collection, the Franklin 
Library was very proud to be accepted for membership in the Maine 
Regional Library System, a coalition of all types of libraries 
throughout the state.  This membership will allow us easier access 
to library materials, opportunities to acquire books and electronic 
services at lower prices, and the benefit of professional interaction 
with other Maine libraries.  As part of the preparation for applying 
for membership, we cataloged the materials in our Library 
collection, made that information available online, and automated 
our circulation system using new barcoded library cards for our 
patrons.  Another piece of good news in March was an award from

Registrar of Voters 
 
The Registrar of V oters is responsible for making sure all registered 
voters for the Town of Franklin are kept current in our file system 
and in CVR (Central V oter Registration). 
 
All registered voter cards are kept in a small file – changing often.  
New voters are registered in person, by mail or through the Motor 
Vehicle Division of the State of Maine.  Current voters occasionally 
change their name, address or party enrollment and we of course 
lose voters due to moving or death. 
 
A number of days before each election an up-to-date Incoming 
V oter List must be presented to our Town Clerk.  These are the 
voting lists you see at the Ballot clerk's tables whenever you vote.  
New voters, of course, can register and/or enroll in a party of their 
choice on election day with proof of residency and an ID.  The 
Registrar must be there with the proper cards. 
 
Any statewide or local “petition” signed by any person declaring 
Franklin as their residence must be sent to the Registrar to check 
each voter's signature and return each petition to the sender. 
 
Currently we have a total of 1120 registered voters, 364 Un-
enrolled, 298 Democrats, 416 Republicans, and 42 Green 
Independent. 
 
The Registrar is appointed by the Selectboard every two years.  I 
appreciate this appointment and sincerely enjoy it.

University of Maine Center 
For Cooperative Aquaculture Research 
 
2018 Annual report for the Town of Franklin 
 
The University of Maine Center for Cooperative Aquaculture 
(CCAR) entered its 18th year of operation in 2018.  Located across 
from Butler Point in upper Taunton Bay, the CCAR is Maine’ 
largest aquaculture research and development facility. 
 
Along with the neighboring USDA National Cold-Water Marine 
Aquaculture Center, where salmon are bred for the aquaculture 
industry, Franklin is home to one of the largest aquaculture research 
campuses in the country. 
 
The CCAR provides space and expertise for companies to start up 
projects, run trials, and demonstrate new technologies.  Our 
hatcheries have raised juvenile sea urchins, California yellowtail, 
halibut, cod and other marine fish for companies to grow to market 
or for research. 
 
Two companies now use CCAR facilities for their operations.  Sea 
& Reef Aquaculture has grown to be the 2nd largest marine 
ornamental fish breeder in the US during its seven years operation 
in Franklin.  The company has seven full-time employees working 
in a 10,000 ft2 business incubator building, where they raise 
clownfish, dottybacks, cardinalfish and other tropical aquarium fish.   
The fish are distributed all over the US and internationally.   
 
Our other company is American Unagi, which is building a 
commercial eel farm in mid-coast Maine.  American Unagi is 
growing eels sourced from the Maine fishery in a CCAR tank room 
as their farm gets constructed, and they make a delicious smoked 
eel product!  
 
In addition to these companies the CCAR has research programs for 
seaweed aquaculture, sea urchins, sandworms, California

yellowtail, and other species.  Our crew of eight is on call 24/7 to 
ensure the fish are safe and properly cared for. 
 
We want to take this opportunity to thank the citizens of Franklin, 
especially the volunteer Fire Department and our neighbors both 
here in Franklin and in Sullivan. 
 
The CCAR offers public tours by appointment.  More information 
can be found at our website https://umaine.edu/cooperative-
aquaculture/
