History MuseumForsyth, Georgia
From the arrival of the “Ocmulgee” 
in 1838 to the last stop of the “Nancy 
Hanks” in 1971, railroads have played 
a critical role in the history of Forsyth 
and Monroe County. Today, the Monroe 
County Historical Society celebrates that 
heritage with its museum and archives 
located in two historic depots.
 
The earlier depot, made of stone 
and constructed in 1858, was the 
disembarking point for wounded and 
sick soldiers brought to the Confederate 
hospitals in Forsyth. In the building with 
its freight scales, bales of cotton were sent 
to national and international markets in 
the 19th century. Through its doors later 
came raw materials for Forsyth’s nearby 
textile industries. 
The second depot, constructed of brick under court order after 
Forsyth citizens sued the railroad, was finished in 1899. The Victorian 
Depot was designed by Bruce and Morgan, the celebrated Atlanta 
architectural firm, who also designed the Monroe County Courthouse.
In addition, a baggage room was 
constructed in 1917 to handle the luggage 
of the many passengers who came to and 
left from Forsyth for all parts of the United 
States. In the early 20th century, about 
25 passenger and freight trains passed 
through Forsyth daily.  Today, Norfolk 
Southern freight trains frequently pass but 
do not stop.

There is no charge to see the exhibits or to use the 
research materials. Donations are always appreciated.
After the railroad no 
longer used it, the Forsyth 
Women’s Club secured 
a lease on the brick 
depot. The Depot Players, 
a community theater 
group, used the building 
for such plays as “Wait Until Dark” and “Our 
Town.” With the organization of the Monroe 
County Historical Society in 1975, the 
building became the home for its archival 
and artifact collections. Collections range 
from prehistoric and Native American 
artifacts to civil rights memorabilia to 
current events. 
Today, visitors can explore this brick depot’s exhibits reflecting the 
history of Forsyth and Monroe County. The society’s archival collection 
is open to researchers interested in Monroe County and local family 
history. 
The stone depot provides a meeting place for the Historical Society and 
various other groups. It also houses a display of memorabilia reflecting 
Bessie Tift College for women, that was across the railroad tracks from 
the depots under multiple names from 1849 until its closure in 1987.
