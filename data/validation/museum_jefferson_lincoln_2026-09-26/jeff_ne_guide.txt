FLATHEAD WMA: Located one mile south of Fairbury 
along the banks of the Little Blue River, has 250 acres 
ideal for hiking, bird watching, horseback riding, 
hunting, fishing and camping. No open fires. Open year-
round. Deer, dove, quail, rabbit, waterfowl, squirrel, 
turkey and channel catfish. No rifles or handguns are 
allowed. Frederick Street and Crystal Springs Road, 
Fairbury.
ROCK GLEN WMA: Located just south of Rock Creek 
Station, 6 miles east and 2 miles south of Fairbury, has 
707 acres, making it ideal for hiking, bird watching, 
horseback riding, hunting and camping. No open fires.  
Deer, dove, quail, grouse, rabbit, squirrel and turkey.  
709 Road and 576 Avenue, Fairbury.
ROSE CREEK WMA: Located two miles south, 
two and one half miles west, one and a quarter mile 
south of Fairbury, has 384 acres of wood-studded 
ravines, croplands, prairie grasses ideal for hiking, 
bird watching, horseback riding, hunting, fishing and  
camping. No open fires. Deer, pheasant, dove, quail, 
rabbit, squirrel, turkey,  waterfowl,channel catfish, 
carp and bullheads. Target shooting is allowed on the 
wildlife management area but banned within 100 yards 
of the parking lot. 
ROSE CREEK WEST WMA: Located two miles south, 
three and one half miles west, 2 miles south of Fairbury, 
280 acres ideal for hiking, bird watching, horseback 
riding, hunting, fishing and camping. No open fires. 
LEISURE LAKE: Leisure Lake is a primitive lake located 
three miles south and one half mile east of Plymouth. 
The lake that offers hiking, fishing and hunting. The lake 
is stocked with largemouth bass, bluegill and channel 
catfish. 720 Road and 580 Avenue, Plymouth.

MCNISH PARK
Originally founded in 1920, McNish Park was called Highland 
Park and was located at 12th and J Streets. It was later expanded 
from the original block and a half when Mrs. Sylvia McNish 
bequeathed both money and land to the City of Fairbury to 
build a park on the opposite side of town from the City Park. 
The timeliness of the bequest fit well with the plans to use WPA 
(Workers Progress Administration) funds and labor to expand the 
park. Once construction was complete in 1941 it was dedicated 
and renamed McNish Park.
The park has several man-made ponds with rock formations, 
waterfalls and fountains. A nine hole Disc Golf course meanders 
through the park grounds. The course, which was planned by a 
disc golf pro, Brad Packett, helps make the course a challenge 
for newcomers and veterans of the sport. The course wanders 
through the mature trees, ponds and waterfalls presenting 
different levels of difficulty to the course.
