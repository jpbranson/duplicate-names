History
History:
Hiwan’s story begins in 1893, when Dr. Mary Josepha “Jo” Williams Douglas and her mother, Mary Neosho Bailey Williams, purchased land in Evergreen, Colorado, to create a summer retreat they named Camp Neosho. They worked with master craftsman, John “Jock” Spence, whose remarkable carpentry transformed a simple barn into a grand log cabin. Over several decades, Jock added towers, finely detailed woodwork, and numerous outbuildings. Some of the original outbuildings still define the site’s character today.
Dr. Jo was one of the earliest women to earn a medical degree in Colorado. She graduated from Gross Medical College in Denver in 1889, opening the Marquette-Williams Sanitarium in 1891 with Dr. Madeleine Marquette, a friend and fellow medical school graduate. Together, the women treated tuberculosis patients who flocked to Denver for care at unprecedented rates. They started a nursing training program within the sanitarium, helping equip more women to enter the medical field during the tuberculosis crisis.
Episcopal priest and composer Father Charles Winfred Douglas visited the Marquette-Williams Sanitarium on a ministry visit where he was introduced to Dr. Jo. Though she called him an “awkward young man” upon their initial meeting, they married at the sanitarium in June of 1896. Victorian society found it improper for a wealthy, married woman to work, pressuring Dr. Jo to close the sanitarium. No longer working in Denver, The Douglas Family relocated to Camp Neosho full-time. Dr. Jo gave birth to their son, Eric Douglas, there in 1897.
The mountain retreat soon became a lively center of Evergreen’s early cultural and religious activity. The family shaped the Evergreen community through their support of local worship, music, philanthropy, and education. They travelled together extensively throughout the American Southwest collecting Indigenous art. Their son Eric became a groundbreaking curator at the Denver Art Museum, playing a key role in elevating Indigenous art within American museum practice.
After Dr. Jo passed away in 1938, the property was sold to Oklahoma oilman and rancher Darst Buchanan. Darst and his wife Ruth renamed the property Hiwan Ranch in 1939 and expanded it to more than 15,000 acres, establishing one of the top Hereford cattle operations in the nation. Their herd earned national recognition, fueled by significant investments including a record-setting purchase of a $61,000 bull. Ranch life at Hiwan thrived for decades, and the family’s influence reached far beyond the barbed wire property lines.
By the 1950s, the Buchanan family became central figures in shaping modern Evergreen. Darst and his son-in-law, John Casey, founded the Hiwan Development Company, which established Evergreen's first major residential developments, including the Hiwan Hills neighborhood and Hiwan Golf Club. These projects brought essential infrastructure to the mostly summer-use town, such as water and sewer services, turning Evergreen into the vibrant year-round community seen today.
When Hiwan’s historic structures faced redevelopment threats in the early 1970s, Evergreen residents and the Jefferson County Historical Society rallied to save Hiwan's landmarks. In response to the community, Jefferson County Parks & Open Space purchased the property in 1974, opening Hiwan Heritage Park & Museum in 1975. Today, Hiwan Museum preserves and shares the histories of the families who called Camp Neosho and Hiwan Ranch home, connecting visitors to the people, places, and community efforts that shaped Evergreen across generations.

ADA Information:
Due to the historic nature of Hiwan Heritage Park and Museum buildings and grounds, full ADA accessibility is not available. Some areas of the home are accessible to visitors, and for those unable to access the full site, we offer ADA binders with photographs and content covering the inaccessible spaces. We are committed to making our history as welcoming and inclusive as possible and are happy to assist with accommodations. Please contact us in advance with any questions about accessibility or available resources.

Volunteer At Hiwan
Volunteer at the Hiwan Museum!
The JCPOS Cultural Resources Team is excited to announce new volunteer opportunities as we embark on an exciting revitalization of Evergreen’s beloved Hiwan Museum and Heritage Park. Our vision for this historic site includes enriching the visitor experience with an updated tour and expanded program offerings and community engagement—but we can’t do it without you.
Hiwan Museum Docent
Help share the stories of Hiwan by guiding visitors through the museum, engaging them with the history of the site, and helping the experience be engaging through interpretation.
Museum Steward
The Museum Steward Volunteer provides support for various tasks throughout the Hiwan Museum and Heritage Park outbuildings. Possible assignments include helping with events and programming, gallery attending, cleaning, and restocking supplies.
History Educator-School Programs
History Educators provide engaging and educational cultural programming to school visitors of various ages on field trips to the Hiwan Museum and assist with family programming such as cultural commemorations and holiday events.
